Privacy

monogatari website · plain-language summary of what happens to your information

What monogatari is

A place to write the chapters of your life beside your own photographs. It is a static website with no monogatari server: no database of users, no analytics, no advertising, no tracking.

Your writing

When you sign in with Google, your story is saved to a hidden folder in your own Google Drive that only monogatari can open (Google's "application data folder"). monogatari asks Google for exactly one Drive permission, drive.appdata, which cannot see or change any of your other Drive files. Nobody else, including the people who made monogatari, can read your story.

Until a change has reached your Drive (for example, if you're offline), it is kept temporarily in this browser's storage and removed as soon as it's saved. Signing out lets you discard anything not yet saved.

Your photos

Photos you choose are read inside your browser to find the date each was taken. They are never uploaded, copied, or sent anywhere, and are not saved to your Drive. Browsers don't keep access to your folders between visits.

Your Google account

Signing in shares your name, email address, and profile picture with the page so it can show who is signed in. Your Google sign-in session lives only in the open page and is never stored. This browser remembers only your name and email, to offer "Continue as …" next time; signing out removes that too.

Removing everything

  • Sign out of monogatari (top-right account button).
  • In Google Drive, open Settings → Manage apps → monogatari → Options → "Delete hidden app data" to delete your story, then "Disconnect from Drive".
  • Remove monogatari's access at myaccount.google.com/connections.

Not a medical service

monogatari supports reflection; it isn't therapy and isn't an emergency service. If you might be in danger, call or text 988 (US & Canada), 116 123 (UK & Ireland), or your local emergency number.
