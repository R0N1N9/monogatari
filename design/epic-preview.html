A House, a Leaving, and the Long Way Home

Thirty-one years, told in the order they happened — and slowly re-known.

Spring 1996 · age 4

The House on Alder Street

The kitchen was always warm and always loud. I remember the light more than anything — late afternoon coming sideways through the window over the sink, turning the dust into something like snow. My grandmother hummed while she worked. I did not know yet that a childhood could be a place you carry, not just a time you leave.

Autumn 2011 · age 19

Leaving

I packed the car in the dark so no one would see me cry. For a long time I told this as a story about escape. Writing it now, I can see it was also a story about grief — that you can need to go and still lose something real in the going. Both things were true. I am allowed to hold both.

Summer 2023 · age 31

Small Mercies

These days the healing is quiet and unglamorous. A morning walk. A friend who texts back. Learning that rest is not a reward I have to earn. The old story said I was someone things happened to. The truer story, the one I am re-knowing here, is that I am also someone who kept choosing to begin again.

物語 · Chapter Two

Leaving

I packed the car in the dark so no one would see me cry. For a long time I told this as a story about escape — the night I finally got free, the engine turning over like a held breath let go. It is a good story. It is also not the whole of it.

Writing it here, years on, I can see what the younger telling left out. That you can need to go and still lose something real in the going. That grief and relief can share a single breath. For most of my life I thought I had to choose which one was true. I am learning that I don't.

This is what the re-knowing is for. Not to rewrite what happened, but to read it again with older, kinder eyes — and to find, in the margins, the parts of me that were quietly keeping me alive.
